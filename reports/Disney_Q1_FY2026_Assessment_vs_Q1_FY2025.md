# Disney: Q1 FY2026 outcome vs Q1 FY2025 internal assessment

Executive summary

Disney's top-line performance remained solid year-over-year, with revenue rising to $26.0bn (+5% YoY) compared with the Q1 FY2025 internal assessment. However, profitability and cash conversion weakened meaningfully: adjusted EPS fell ~7% to $1.63, segment operating income decreased ~10% to $4.6bn, and free cash flow swung to a -$2.28bn outflow. Streaming profitability improved but subscriber transparency was reduced as the company stopped disclosing quarterly paid subscribers. (Source: Consolidated findings provided by user)

Original research question / hypothesis

The original hypothesis was that Disney was improving overall but faced material risks: streaming churn and ARPU pressure, sports rights cost headwinds, potential parks softness (including weather sensitivity), and a delayed recovery in free cash flow due to cruise and capex timing. (Source: Consolidated findings provided by user)

Key findings

- Revenue: Q1 FY2026 revenue was $26.0bn, up 5% YoY and in line with the prior Q1 FY2025 internal assessment that flagged a 5% YoY increase. (Source: Consolidated findings provided by user)

- Adjusted EPS: Adjusted EPS declined to $1.63 in Q1 FY2026, down ~7% from the $1.76 (a +44% YoY view) in the Q1 FY2025 internal assessment, indicating that profit momentum did not carry through. (Source: Consolidated findings provided by user)

- Total segment operating income: Segment operating income declined by ~10% to $4.6bn in Q1 FY2026 versus the $5.1bn (+31% YoY) internal assessment for Q1 FY2025, reflecting weaker margins. (Source: Consolidated findings provided by user)

- Entertainment operating income: Entertainment OI fell ~35% to $1.1bn in Q1 FY2026 versus the $1.7bn (+95% YoY) internal assessment, representing the largest disappointment relative to prior strength. (Source: Consolidated findings provided by user)

- Streaming / DTC operating income and subscribers: Streaming profitability improved (Entertainment SVOD OI reported at $450mm versus a prior internal Streaming/DTC OI of $293mm), but the company stopped disclosing quarterly paid subscriber counts for Disney+ and Hulu beginning in FY26; Disney+ had been reported at 124.6mm (slight q/q decline) and Hulu at 53.6mm (+3% q/q) in the prior internal assessment. (Source: Consolidated findings provided by user)

- Sports operating income: Sports OI was weaker than the internal mix implied (prior implied ~ $250mm) and realized at $191mm in Q1 FY2026, indicating materialized pressure likely driven by rights costs and margin drag. (Source: Consolidated findings provided by user)

- Experiences (Parks & Experiences): Experiences OI was resilient, rising by ~7% to $3.3bn in Q1 FY2026 versus a $3.1bn internal assessment for Q1 FY2025, consistent with prior 6–8% growth framing. (Source: Consolidated findings provided by user)

- Cash flow and capex: Operating cash flow deteriorated sharply to $735mm (versus an internal $3.2bn forecast). Free cash flow swung from an expected $739mm to -$2.28bn in Q1 FY2026, a key deterioration attributed to higher capex (actual $3.0bn vs prior $2.5bn) and cruise-related investment timing. (Source: Consolidated findings provided by user)

Bottom line

The Q1 FY2025 assessment accurately identified an improving revenue trajectory but flagged key risks that materialized into Q1 FY2026: while revenue and Experiences held up and streaming profitability improved, broader profitability and cash conversion weakened. The primary divergence from the more optimistic FY25 setup was in margin durability and cash conversion rather than revenue growth. (Source: Consolidated findings provided by user)
