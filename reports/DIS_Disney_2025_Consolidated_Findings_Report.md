Short title

Disney (DIS) 2025: Shift from Turnaround to Multi-Pillar Recovery

Executive summary

Across 2025 our internal assessment of Disney (DIS) remained an Outperform stance while conviction steadily increased as the company’s recovery became demonstrable across multiple businesses. Early-year views shifted from a turnaround driven mainly by cost cuts to a durable earnings recovery led by DTC profitability and Experiences (parks, cruise). By year-end management’s guidance and results showed material DTC operating income, record Experiences operating income, improved free cash flow, and larger capital returns, supporting a higher valuation and price target progression. The principal cautions through the year were linear TV declines, ESPN rights cost pressure, studio slate volatility, and elevated capex.[1]

Original research question / hypothesis

Hypothesis: Disney will move from a cost-cutting turnaround to a multi-pillar earnings recovery in 2025, led by a material DTC/streaming profit inflection and continued strength in Parks & Experiences, producing sustainable EPS growth and improved capital returns.[1]

Key findings (chronological)

- Feb. 5, 2025 — Q1 FY25 note: Rating Outperform, $115 PT. The thesis shifted from primarily “turnaround/cost cuts” to a real earnings recovery led by DTC profitability and studio improvement; DTC operating income inflected positive and Parks remained the earnings anchor, with studio benefiting from theatrical hits and Sports/ESPN improving after Star India deconsolidation. FY25 EPS growth was expected in the high single digits.[1]

- Q2 FY25 note — quarter ended Mar. 29, 2025: Rating Outperform, $120 PT. Conviction increased after a guidance raise, better streaming economics, and stronger Experiences trends. Drivers included improved DTC subscribers/ARPU, streaming losses turning into profit, strong domestic parks and cruise results, and Content Sales swinging profitable. The note stated: “Guidance raise, DTC profit step-up, and Experiences strength offset sports cost timing.”[1]

- Aug. 6, 2025 — Q3 FY25 note: Rating Outperform, $125 PT. The team concluded DTC profitability was scaling rather than only inflecting; the recovery thesis emphasized DTC + Experiences as drivers while studio and linear remained volatile risks. Streaming profit became material, Experiences continued to grow, FY25 adjusted EPS guidance/target moved higher to around $5.85, and valuation used roughly 19x FY26E EPS of ~$6.55.[1]

- Nov. 13, 2025 — Q4 / FY25 note: Rating Outperform, $135 PT. This was the most constructive point: meaningful DTC profit, record Experiences operating income, improved FCF, and stronger capital returns were all observable. FY DTC operating income reached about $1.3B, Experiences operating income hit roughly $10B, and management guided to double-digit FY26 EPS growth with larger buybacks/dividends. Remaining risks included studio volatility, linear declines, ESPN cost pressure, and elevated cruise/parks capex. Valuation moved to roughly 19x FY27E EPS of ~$7.10.[1]

Bottom line

- Stance: Outperform maintained throughout 2025 with steadily rising conviction.[1]
- Price target progression: $115 → $120 → $125 → $135 (Feb → Q2 → Aug → Nov notes).[1]
- Primary evolution in thesis: from a cost-cutting turnaround story to a multi-pillar earnings recovery driven by (1) DTC/streaming profit inflection becoming material, (2) Parks, Experiences, and cruise remaining the core earnings engine, (3) improving guidance and EPS visibility, and (4) better FCF and capital return outlook by year-end.[1]
- Main cautions repeated across notes: linear TV decline, ESPN rights costs, studio volatility, and elevated capex (especially for cruise/parks).[1]
