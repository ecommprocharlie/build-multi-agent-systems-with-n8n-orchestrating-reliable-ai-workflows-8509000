# Disney FY25 Internal Assessment — Evolution of View and Key Drivers

Executive summary

The internal view on Disney became steadily more constructive through 2025, with ratings maintained at Outperform while price targets rose from $115 to $135 as streaming profitability and Experiences strength increased (Q1–Q4 FY25 notes). Management raised guidance and signaled more shareholder-friendly capital return actions by year-end, supporting the improved outlook (Q2 FY25 note; Q4 FY25 note). Persistent risks cited across the year included studio volatility, ESPN/sports-rights inflation, cord-cutting, macro sensitivity, and elevated capex (consolidated notes Q1–Q4 FY25).

Original research question / hypothesis

Did Disney demonstrate a durable turnaround in FY25—driven principally by direct-to-consumer (DTC) streaming profitability and resilient Experiences—that justifies maintaining an Outperform rating and progressively higher price targets?

Key findings

- Rating and price-target trajectory: The internal rating remained Outperform throughout 2025 while the price target progressed from $115 → $120 → $125 → $135 over the four notes (Q1 FY25 note; Q2 FY25 note; Q3 FY25 note; Q4 FY25 note).

- Overall tenor: Views shifted from "constructive but cautious" early in the year to steadily higher confidence mid-year and a largely validated turnaround by year-end (Q1 FY25 note; Q2 FY25 note; Q3 FY25 note; Q4 FY25 note).

- DTC / streaming: DTC streaming profitability was a principal driver of the improving view. Early notes flagged emerging signs of DTC profitability; mid- and late-year notes documented scaling DTC operating income and characterized streaming as moving from "improving" to a core earnings driver (Q1 FY25 note; Q2 FY25 note; Q3 FY25 note; Q4 FY25 note). Management targeted about $1.3B of FY25 Entertainment DTC operating income (Q3 FY25 note).

- Experiences (Parks & Cruises): Experiences remained resilient across the year and contributed materially to operating results; by Q4 Experiences delivered record operating income and helped offset other cost pressures (Q1 FY25 note; Q2 FY25 note; Q3 FY25 note; Q4 FY25 note).

- Guidance and earnings progression: Management raised FY25 adjusted EPS guidance to $5.75 mid-year, reinforcing the view that earnings recovery was ahead of plan (Q2 FY25 note). By Q4 the notes indicated the recovery thesis was "largely validated," reflecting expanded DTC profitability and improved sports profitability despite transition costs (Q4 FY25 note).

- Shareholder returns and capital allocation: A material positive late in the year was management's more shareholder-friendly stance, including a $7B buyback target and a $1.50 dividend (Q4 FY25 note).

- Persisting risks: Throughout 2025 the team continued to flag several persistent risks: studio volatility, ESPN/sports-rights inflation, subscriber churn/cord-cutting, macro sensitivity affecting Experiences and broader results, and elevated capex and FCF normalization concerns (Q1 FY25 note; Q2 FY25 note; Q3 FY25 note; Q4 FY25 note).

- Primary drivers of improved sentiment: The notes attribute improved internal sentiment to streaming profitability scaling, repeated guidance raises, resilient parks/cruise performance, stronger cash flow, and renewed shareholder returns (Overall evolution; Q2 FY25 note; Q3 FY25 note; Q4 FY25 note).
